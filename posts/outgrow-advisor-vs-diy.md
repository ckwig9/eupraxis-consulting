<h2>Thinking About Implementing Outgrow? Here&#8217;s What to Know Before You Decide.</h2>

<p>If you&#8217;ve read <em>Outgrow</em> by Alex Goldfayn &#8211; or even just the first chapter &#8211; you’ve likely had a moment of clarity. The system is simple, behavioral, and compelling: daily proactive outreach, tracked over time, to create 15–30 percent annual revenue growth.</p>

<p>But clarity invites a question:</p>

<p><strong>Can I do this myself, or should I work with an Outgrow Advisor?</strong></p>

<p>The short answer:</p>

<p>Yes, you can start on your own.</p>

<p>But if you want to scale the system, embed it culturally, and accelerate results across your team, working with an Advisor might be your best next step, especially if you&#8217;re leading a growth-stage company actively investing in structure and execution.</p>

<p>Let’s walk through the case for both paths.</p>

<h2>Option 1: Start on Your Own (When It Makes Sense)</h2>

<p>If you&#8217;re a founder or leader with a small team &#8211; or if you’re still learning how Outgrow works &#8211; you don’t need anyone’s permission to begin. In fact, starting solo can be the smartest way to:</p>

<ul>
<li>Build initial traction and proof of concept</li>

<li>Test drive the core habits (like Proactive Calls and tracking daily outreach)</li>

<li>Share the ideas organically with your team</li>

<li>Decide if it’s worth investing in company-wide rollout</li>
</ul>

<p>We’ve seen business owners start this way and make real progress, especially when they’re hands-on and energized.</p>

<p>Here’s what a successful DIY start often looks like:</p>

<ol>
<li>Read the book (or listen on Audible)</li>

<li>Commit to 3–5 proactive touches per day</li>

<li>Track those actions visibly (Google Sheet, whiteboard, notebook)</li>

<li>Share results at team meetings</li>
</ol>

<p>This approach isn’t hypothetical. It works. And for solopreneurs, consultants, or leaders with strong internal accountability, it may be all you need.</p>

<p>Just know that the simplicity of Outgrow is easy to underestimate.</p>

<p>The challenge isn’t <em>knowing</em> what to do.</p>

<p>It’s <em>doing</em> it consistently quarter after quarter, especially when your day gets busy.</p>

<h2>Option 2: Work with a Certified Outgrow Advisor (When You Want to Go Further, Faster)</h2>

<p>For companies in growth mode &#8211; particularly those between $1M–$50M in annual revenue &#8211; going solo can become a bottleneck.</p>

<p>That’s where working with an Outgrow Advisor becomes a strategic decision.</p>

<p>Not because you can’t figure it out, but because <strong>your time, team, and momentum are too valuable to waste.</strong></p>

<p>Advisors are trained not only in the core system, but in the rollout strategies that help:</p>

<ul>
<li>Translate the book into a working plan</li>

<li>Equip your team with tools, scripts, and cadences</li>

<li>Coach through mindset shifts and objections</li>

<li>Create visibility into behaviors that drive revenue</li>

<li>Maintain consistency long after the kickoff</li>
</ul>

<p>Think of it like many of our clients who run on the Entrepreneurial Operating System (EOS): You can read the book <a href="https://www.amazon.com/Traction-Get-Grip-Your-Business/dp/1936661837"><em>Traction</em></a>, by Gino Wickman and self-implement, but working with a Certified EOS Implementer almost always leads to faster clarity, cleaner execution, and longer-lasting results.</p>

<p>For founders &amp; CEOs we work with, where team culture and values often matter as much as results, Advisors also help frame the system in ways that feel human and aligned, not high-pressure or robotic.</p>

<h2>What an Advisor Won’t Do</h2>

<p>This isn’t about outsourcing accountability.</p>

<p>Your team still has to pick up the phone.</p>

<p>You still have to lead by example.</p>

<p>But a good Advisor helps keep the system light, clear, and sticky, so you don’t have to reinvent the wheel or carry all the cultural weight alone.</p>

<p>They help you do what you already know is right; more consistently and with less resistance.</p>

<p>Here’s a simple diagnostic:</p>

<p>Starting solo is a great way to build belief.</p>

<p>But scaling company-wide usually requires structure, support, and a few well-placed nudges from the outside.</p>
